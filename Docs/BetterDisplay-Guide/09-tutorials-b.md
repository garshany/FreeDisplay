### 1. XDR/HDR desktop brightness — https://betterdisplay.pro/guide/tutorials/xdr-and-hdr-brightness-upscaling/

- **BetterDisplay behaviour** (Pro for software upscaling/calibration, direct and legacy native methods, shared readiness and neutral keyboard step):
  - Upscaling starts **off**. Require an eligible XDR panel or an HDR-capable external panel already in HDR mode, enabled through the display's App Menu or macOS. SDR-only hardware cannot gain HDR output. Available output depends on preset, content, power and temperature; neither percentages nor highlight peak ratings establish sustained full-screen nits.
  - Software setup lives in Settings → Displays → display → Image Adjustments: enable software video adjustments; use color-table control on supported Apple Silicon paths. Intel needs color-table control off and a compositor/Metal fallback. With color-table and compositor processing both off, upscaling can use Metal automatically even when the separate Metal-effects preference is off. The App Menu's additional options and the per-display software HDR/XDR switch control the same upscaling feature.
  - Use combined brightness when an actual hardware controller is available; otherwise use software brightness. A hardware-dominant main slider may require opening Image Adjustments for the software slider. Software scales can exceed 100%; direct/native hardware scales can still be expressed as 0–100% of their expanded span.
  - Direct control is an **experimental built-in XDR** option under Device Control → Apple brightness. It requires native Apple brightness, compatible presets and HDR peak exceeding SDR peak. External Pro Display XDR and Studio Display XDR use software paths; ordinary Studio Display is not XDR. Enable direct-control availability, disable software upscaling, then activate the direct App Menu action or direct Apple preset. BetterDisplay must handle brightness keys because system keys use a different scale.
  - Direct entry saves the previous Apple preset, brightness and automatic-brightness state, turns automatic brightness off, and restores those states on exit. Additional Apple Brightness Settings exposes the nits bounds; the shown unchanged default is **0–1600 nits**, with Force EDR **off**. Disable the active action to exit, and disable its availability switch separately if desired. Panel power/thermal limiting still applies.
  - Legacy native XDR uses a special Apple preset plus automatic-activation and minimum/maximum-nits settings. It is unavailable on **macOS 26.3+**. Leaving it requires the ordinary preset and automatic activation off.
  - For HDR fidelity on the color-table path, software-only **100% is neutral**, or use the combined slider's hardware-only interval between minimum and maximum hardware markers with other effects neutral. The illustrated minimum of 20%, maximum of 100%, and 125%/124% examples are configuration values, not universal defaults. Shared brightness settings provide an intermediate neutral keyboard/OSD stop, continuous readiness, and color reset at neutral; the screenshot shows all three enabled, without establishing all fresh-install defaults. Keyboard → OSD selects feedback and percentage/nits readouts. Color reset may flicker or black out briefly; allow disabling it. Metal avoids this particular color-table clipping mechanism but can still overbrighten HDR.
  - Calibration is under Image Adjustments → Calibrate Upscaling with HDR/upscaling active. Offer auto calibration for Apple preset-capable displays and manual near-white discrimination with save/discard. If the target disappears at the minimum, correct hardware contrast/gamma first. Temporarily neutralize software effects and disable Night Shift/True Tone; restore those effects on either exit, but leave software/combined brightness neutral even after discard. Calibration is not direct-range configuration or measured tone-mapping validation.
  - Continuous readiness trades background load for smoother entry; turning it off can slow activation. Investigate ambient-auto-brightness/headroom changes when performance fluctuates. Undo restores changed HDR mode, calibration/ranges, automatic brightness and other effects separately. Verify clipping and brightness on the panel, not screenshots.
- **FreeDisplay today**: **🟡 Partial**. Built-in-only eligibility checks potential EDR headroom at `Apps/OpenDisplay/Sources/AppModel.swift:785`. A small Metal EDR window is implemented at `Apps/OpenDisplay/Sources/XDRBrightnessController.swift:76`; desktop brightening itself multiplies gamma at `Apps/OpenDisplay/Sources/AppModel.swift:719`. This is not a full-desktop Metal adjustment pipeline or direct nits control. The policy caps boost at 3.2× and uses a default fraction of 1/2.2 (`Packages/TopologyCore/Sources/TopologyCore/XDRBrightness.swift:18`). Labs and tray expose it, with HDR clipping/heat warnings and power automation (`Apps/OpenDisplay/Sources/SettingsView.swift:750`, `Apps/OpenDisplay/Sources/MenuBarView.swift:317`). External HDR upscaling, calibrated limits, neutral hardware/software range composition, direct control and legacy preset management are **⬜ Missing**. Advanced tools only report EDR, explicitly without enabling hardware HDR (`Apps/OpenDisplay/Sources/AdvancedDisplayToolsView.swift:24`).
- **Implementation notes**: **L; medium/high API risk.** Public AppKit/Metal EDR and CoreGraphics transfer tables support the present approach; they do not prove HDR fidelity. Add per-display capability/range models before widening support. Direct Apple brightness/preset control needs private DisplayServices/CoreDisplay-style research and OS gating; legacy support is a separate, low-priority compatibility decision. Night Shift/True Tone manipulation also needs capability-specific integration. Design original calibration imagery and restore transactions. Backlog TB-17–19.
- **Acceptance criteria**:
  - Unsupported/SDR displays never advertise usable upscaling; hardware and software percentages name their range and method.
  - Verify neutral, low and high SDR plus HDR highlights physically; reset/discard/disable restores the documented state.
  - Test power transitions, preset/headroom changes, quit and topology changes without stale gamma boost or abandoned EDR windows.
  - Direct and software modes cannot conflict; direct exit restores saved auto-brightness/preset/level. Legacy controls are absent on unsupported systems.

### 2. Hardware brightness floor, dithering and limited-color appearance — https://betterdisplay.pro/guide/tutorials/reduce-pwm-and-temporal-dithering/

- **BetterDisplay behaviour** (Pro: hardware floor and quantization; Free: basic software dimming, exposed GPU dithering/framebuffer appearance; custom virtual alternatives and temperature retain Pro requirements):
  - Separate backlight/pixel PWM, GPU temporal dithering and a panel's internal processing. None of these settings promises a modulation-free display or relief from eye strain. Record initial settings, vary one control at a time and use panel documentation/measurement; screenshots cannot verify modulation.
  - Image Adjustments must have software video processing and combined brightness enabled with an active hardware controller. Image Adjustment Settings accepts a **0–99% hardware floor**, expressed on that controller's scale, not the combined slider's scale. Below the floor, software dimming supplies darkness while hardware stays at or above it. The illustrated 10% is not a recommended PWM threshold. Clear the custom floor to return to default; the page does not state a numeric default.
  - Independent hardware sliders, other apps, monitor auto brightness and the monitor OSD can bypass this floor. Without working hardware control, set the backlight manually and use software dimming. Dimming can change contrast, black appearance and precision.
  - App Menu → display → Color Mode exposes GPU Dithering only when the Apple Silicon framebuffer setting can be read. Toggle off, verify reported off state and inspect gradients for banding. The setting is remembered per display; panel FRC may continue. Consider the separate Uniformity Correction interaction on supported built-in panels.
  - Intel Color Depth may offer 8-bit; Apple Silicon Color Mode may offer an 8-bit connection format. Availability depends on mode/refresh/connection; inspect SDR/HDR and encoding together. Selecting 8-bit is independent of disabling dithering. A virtual SDR source does not guarantee a dithering-free physical output, and virtual screens can themselves offer HDR.
  - Image Adjustments → Quantization reduces levels deliberately; the example is five levels through compositor processing and also affects captured output. Supported Apple Silicon Color Mode → Grayscale Appearance removes chroma. Combine if desired, but neither proves modulation stopped. Undo with Standard Appearance, a separate quantization reset, the original format and original dithering state.
- **FreeDisplay today**: **🟡 Partial** dimming, **⬜ Missing** controller floor, dithering/encoding controls, quantization and framebuffer grayscale. `Packages/TopologyCore/Sources/TopologyCore/Dimming.swift:4` defines gamma, overlay and their combination; “combined” here means **gamma plus overlay**, not hardware plus software. It has a 0.15 gamma floor and 0.9 overlay-opacity cap (`:20`, `:23`). The brightness route chooses native/DDC/software individually (`Apps/OpenDisplay/Sources/AppModel.swift:1374`). The capture LUT/transform path is confined to the preview (`Providers/CaptureProvider/Sources/CaptureImageTransform.swift:6`), not a desktop dithering feature. Current mode enumeration exists at `Providers/CoreGraphicsProvider/Sources/CoreGraphicsProvider.swift:436`; it is not an Apple Silicon connection-format editor.
- **Implementation notes**: **M** for a shared hardware-floor composer, **L/high risk** for private framebuffer dithering/format control, **M** for original quantization/grayscale rendering. Reuse the DDC/native adapters but distinguish requested hardware minimum from software minimum. Public Core Image/Metal can provide appearance effects in a stream; framebuffer-wide effects require an explicitly supported path. Backlog TB-20–22.
- **Acceptance criteria**:
  - Lowering combined brightness never requests hardware below the configured controller floor; changing the independent hardware slider is clearly outside that policy.
  - Hidden/unsupported dithering controls cannot claim off; reconnect preserves a confirmed supported setting.
  - Quantization/grayscale reset independently; banding and capture effects are documented, with no medical or modulation guarantee.

### 3. Portrait Sidecar presentation — https://betterdisplay.pro/guide/tutorials/rotated-sidecar/

- **BetterDisplay behaviour** (Pro: matching, association, streaming and transforms; stopping a stream remains Free):
  - First establish Sidecar as a separate extended **landscape** desktop; hide its sidebar and Touch Bar. If rotating the iPad breaks/crops native Sidecar, reconnect in landscape before configuring the workaround. Apple device/account/connection prerequisites still apply.
  - Grant BetterDisplay Screen Recording and retain another usable desktop. Displays Overview → Create New Virtual Screen → match/associate to a display selects Sidecar. Keep association and full-screen stream configuration enabled; choose portrait **90° with port down** or **270° with port up**. Create the source and place working windows there. After a permission interruption, reopen creation and inspect existing definitions before retrying to avoid duplicates.
  - The virtual source owns the portrait-shaped desktop; Sidecar presents its rotated image. Adjust source resolution for text size. Later switching orientation requires both source Rotated Orientation and independent stream **0/90/180/270°** rotation to agree; one changes geometry, the other presentation direction.
  - Command-click or the stream's Warp Mouse Cursor action relocates the pointer to the corresponding source point. Place the source beside the primary working screen and Sidecar farther away. Option or Shift reveals Prevent Cursor Entering for the target; verify the exclusion after enabling it.
  - For latency, try USB Sidecar, smaller source resolution or lower Target FPS. Two rendering/transport layers add work. Stop the stream before cleanup, disable association auto-streaming to prevent restart, and optionally discard the saved source through Manage Virtual Screen or its Settings confirmation. Sidecar disconnect remains a separate macOS action; move windows first.
- **FreeDisplay today**: **🟡 Partial building blocks; ⬜ Missing workflow**. Custom dimensions/HiDPI creation exists (`Apps/OpenDisplay/Sources/VirtualDisplaysSettingsView.swift:31`) and preview rotation exists (`Apps/OpenDisplay/Sources/CapturePanel.swift:201`). Capture is one capped SDR window (`Apps/OpenDisplay/Sources/CapturePanel.swift:72`, `:128`), not a target-selected full-screen stream. No Sidecar matching, source association, pointer warp or target cursor barrier is represented in those models. Virtual endpoints are session-owned (`Providers/VirtualDisplayProvider/Sources/VirtualDisplayProvider.swift:25`).
- **Implementation notes**: **L; high inherited API risk.** Private `CGVirtualDisplay` supplies the source; public ScreenCaptureKit, Core Image/Metal and AppKit can capture, transform and present it. Implement source-to-target coordinate inversion using logical/backing dimensions, rotation and fit. Cursor relocation can use CoreGraphics; cursor exclusion is a separate event-handling feature with permission/recovery implications. Never describe this as native portrait Sidecar. TB-23–26.
- **Acceptance criteria**:
  - Both portrait choices show upright, uncropped content with sidebar/Touch Bar hidden; switching back also updates geometry.
  - Permission denial creates no duplicate source; target loss stops presentation safely.
  - Command-click maps corners correctly at each rotation and scale; emergency stop remains available on the recovery display.

### 4. Use an intact rectangle on a damaged panel — https://betterdisplay.pro/guide/tutorials/use-the-working-part-of-a-damaged-screen/

- **BetterDisplay behaviour** (Pro: custom geometry/modes, streaming, mapping and associations; Free: preset definition and stream stop):
  - Determine a usable pixel rectangle; edge damage is the suitable case, whereas a central fault may leave none. This masks unused output black; it neither repairs hardware nor turns off light from damaged pixels.
  - Keep another tested recovery route, grant Screen Recording, and first configure/test Keyboard Shortcuts → Stop all full screen streams. Keep automatic resume off until verified. Shift at app launch enters safe mode and suppresses automatic virtual connection/restoration if needed.
  - Create a preconfigured source in Displays Overview, initially disconnected. Set aspect ratio to the working rectangle or enable a custom resolution list. Enable HiDPI as desired: **960×720 logical becomes 1920×1440 backing pixels**. Below-720p logical modes require the global advanced lower-resolution option; tiny desktops may not fit system dialogs. Save geometry, connect, and use Reconnect if later changes require it.
  - On the source, Stream Virtual Screen selects the damaged physical target. Keep Free Aspect Ratio off. Use 1:1 Pixel Mapping only when backing size matches the available area; otherwise fit with Zoom/Underscan. Horizontal/vertical alignment places output away from damage: right for left-edge damage, top for bottom damage, bottom for top damage. The sample uses right/center alignment. Cropping selects source content and is not a target exclusion rectangle.
  - Check menu bar, dialogs and all four edges with a source window. Command-click returns the pointer from the covered target desktop to the source. Make the source main only after it is visible/operable; arrange the physical target outside the usual pointer path.
  - After recovery and fit tests, enable Resume Stream on Connect or an associated-display rule. Association-managed auto-streaming can disable the separate resume option. Both endpoints must be connected and the receiver cannot already be a mirror target. Reopen menus after granting permission.
  - Undo automatic policies before stopping; move windows to a retained desktop, disconnect to keep the definition or confirm discard, and restore main/layout separately. Smaller logical HiDPI sizes enlarge UI but require rechecking backing size and fit.
- **FreeDisplay today**: **🟡 Partial source/preview controls; ⬜ Missing usable-area presentation**. Definition validation allows 640×480 through 8192×8192 backing dimensions, 24–120 Hz and even HiDPI dimensions (`Packages/DisplayDomain/Sources/DisplayDomain/VirtualDisplayDefinition.swift:18`). Creation connects immediately, with no saved disconnected editor (`Apps/OpenDisplay/Sources/VirtualDisplaysSettingsView.swift:43`). Preview crop is symmetric inset, capped at 40%, and scales proportionally in a floating window (`Providers/CaptureProvider/Sources/CaptureImageTransform.swift:8`, `Apps/OpenDisplay/Sources/CapturePanel.swift:136`). There is no target rectangle, black surrounding surface, pixel mapping, alignment, full-screen stop binding or resumption model.
- **Implementation notes**: **L; high inherited virtual API risk, public presentation APIs.** Build full-screen output and a recovery controller before a damaged-screen wizard. Model source crop separately from destination rectangle, preserve aspect ratio, and calculate with backing pixels rather than assuming points equal pixels. Require a verified usable output before main-display changes. TB-23, TB-25, TB-27.
- **Acceptance criteria**:
  - A 1920×1440 backing image occupies exactly the configured working rectangle at 1:1; target remainder is black.
  - Source menus/dialogs fit; changing HiDPI or zoom cannot silently cover damaged edges.
  - Stop, lost permission, missing target and safe launch all leave a reachable recovery desktop; disabling resume prevents the next automatic start.

### 5. Relative layout and protected main display — https://betterdisplay.pro/guide/tutorials/protect-monitor-arrangement/

- **BetterDisplay behaviour** (Pro: custom groups and all group layout/main enforcement):
  - Require connected, independently positioned extended desktops; mirrored receivers have no independent placement. Keep the app running. Logical desktop bounds, including scaling, determine positions; physical dimensions only influence diagrams/mm labels. This is not app-window or Spaces restoration.
  - Groups Overview creates a named group. New custom groups are enabled, initially use current-display membership, and default to **only members present** and **exclusive** activation. Select explicit left/right members and require all configured members connected. Allow nonmembers if an extra built-in/recovery screen stays connected; review exclusive-group priority. Inspect the active/inactive reason before applying anything. Enabling a group does not capture a layout.
  - Layout Protection starts **off**. Add a general positional rule with an ordinary click: protected display, reference display and side (left/right/above/below). New default anchors are centered. Enable anchor offsets and set both to **0%** for top alignment in a horizontal pair; **50%** returns to center. For vertical placement, offsets become left/right alignment. Hiding offset controls preserves their values. Edits save immediately.
  - One directed relationship is enough for two screens; reciprocal rules can conflict. Option while selecting the group exposes portrait/landscape entries. An orientation-specific rule overrides the general rule **even when the specific rule is disabled**.
  - Apply All Protections acts on eligible rules from **all active groups**, not just the editor's group. Require group/layout/rule enabled, both participants connected and reference/side set; surface missing requirements. Resolving overlap can move a third screen. Reapply relationships when modes change rather than freezing coordinates.
  - Protected main starts unspecified. Select a connected member separately; active group and layout protection are required. Group main wins over individual Main Status protection and can disable a conflicting individual protection. Restore the old individual protection explicitly when undoing that choice.
  - Startup application under Groups Overview → Advanced starts **off**. Tools → Groups → Apply Automatically also starts **off** and concerns menu-based group changes, not every Settings edit. Explicit Apply All remains available. Disabling group/layout/rule prevents future enforcement but does not revert current positions.
  - Distinct names or numbered suffixes do not establish physical identity. Compare UUIDs in Display Information; use per-display Identification & Settings Transfer only when matching is wrong. Basic+extended and Apple-Silicon basic+extended+port can use additional evidence, with port stability required. Global identification defaults apply to new records. Settings transfer overwrites current settings and forgets the old offline record irreversibly; recheck group members/references/main afterwards.
  - Test wake, reconnect and resolution changes independently, including pointer edge crossing. An all-members group is inactive while one is absent. To undo, disable layout only if other group features should remain; remove the specific rule and clear the protected-main selection, restore optional startup/menu flags and prior geometry manually. Discarding a group does not restore arrangement.
- **FreeDisplay today**: **🟡 Partial, different model**. Settings has protected layouts (`Apps/OpenDisplay/Sources/SettingsView.swift:177`), keyed by display-set fingerprints plus managed-offline members, captured as snapshots (`Packages/TopologyCore/Sources/TopologyCore/LayoutProtection.swift:148`, `:196`). Enforcement debounces three seconds and caps attempts at two per stable generation (`:133`, `:138`, `:248`); wake feeds it (`Apps/OpenDisplay/Sources/AppModel.swift:3763`). Group configuration contains brightness/contrast and offsets, not activation/layout policies (`Packages/TopologyCore/Sources/TopologyCore/DisplayGroup.swift:14`). Virtual-class displays are filtered out of protected-layout snapshots (`Packages/TopologyCore/Sources/TopologyCore/LayoutProtection.swift:240`). **Missing**: relational anchors, orientation precedence, group priorities/active reasons, independent main protection and specific protection switches. **P0 code defect**: scene application ignores provider warning results and only reports rotation skipping (`Apps/OpenDisplay/Sources/AppModel.swift:1088` versus `Providers/CoreGraphicsProvider/Sources/CoreGraphicsProvider.swift:371`); protected restores later check residual drift, but manual scene application lacks that reporting.
- **Implementation notes**: **L; low API risk for geometry, higher for lifecycle/rotation.** Public CoreGraphics configuration handles positions/modes/main via origin management. Extend the policy layer with explicit relationships, conflict/cycle handling and main precedence; keep snapshot recovery as a separate feature. Existing registry scoring is a foundation, not an exposed identification-method selector (`Packages/TopologyCore/Sources/TopologyCore/DisplayRegistry.swift:67`). TB-04, TB-09, TB-11–12.
- **Acceptance criteria**:
  - Top-aligned right-of remains adjacent after either display changes logical size; orientation-specific disable suppresses its general rule if adopting this behavior.
  - Inactive groups explain membership/priority failure; all-group application exposes affected displays and conflicts.
  - Main changes, missing members and identical-monitor reconnects preserve the intended physical mapping; disabling protection leaves current geometry intact.
  - Provider failures and skipped fields produce a partial/failed result rather than an apparently clean scene application.

### 6. Explicit Work, Evening and Restore presets — https://betterdisplay.pro/guide/tutorials/work-and-evening-presets/

- **BetterDisplay behaviour** (Free: Shortcuts integration, ordinary modes/basic brightness; Pro: temperature, advanced brightness and relevant group/protection features):
  - The recipes are named macOS Shortcuts on this Mac, not snapshots of previous state. Build and verify Restore first. Keep the app running and both intended desktops connected. Verify each controller separately; omit unsupported temperature/mode/brightness actions consistently from all recipes. Temperature needs software adjustments enabled and not suspended; Night Shift and other effects can combine with it.
  - Record complete available mode combinations: resolution, HiDPI/LoDPI, refresh and depth. Independent lists do not establish every combination. Shortcuts' unspecified parameter leaves that constraint out; mode selection may choose a nearest available result and handle refresh separately. Check the settled display.
  - The example Restore uses 2560×1440 and 1920×1080 HiDPI, 60 Hz/8-bit, brightness 55/45%, neutral temperature. Work keeps those modes with 60/50% brightness. Evening uses 1920×1080 and 1600×900 HiDPI, 60 Hz/8-bit, brightness 25/20%, warmer temperature. These are examples, not required modes/default presets; screenshots use different available modes/levels.
  - Set Brightness stores a normalized 0–1 value. Its include-upscaling-range switch defaults **on**; switch it **off** for ordinary 0–100% requests. With a 0–200% total span and that option on, a 60% parameter requests 120% brightness. It changes interpretation, not whether upscaling is enabled. Restore above-100% values by normalizing against the full range, with unchanged controller/HDR/range settings.
  - Image Adjustment normalizes each feature across its full span: temperature **50% is neutral/menu 0%**, **65% becomes menu +30%**. Conversion from menu percentage is `50 + menuValue / 2`; menu −20% becomes Shortcuts 40%. These are adjustment values, not kelvin.
  - Sequence two mode actions, two brightness actions, then two temperature actions, all with explicit display selections. Mode completion may precede physical settlement; insert a Wait after modes if necessary, starting at **two seconds** and increasing as observed. Duplicate Restore into Work and then Evening. Use absolute values and explicit On/Off, not offsets, dim/brighten increments or unspecified toggles. An explicit image-adjustment On resumes saved effects.
  - Pause applicable sync rules for independent levels; a later display action may otherwise overwrite an earlier one or affect a third display. Review layout/UI-scale rules and saved mode-protection targets: app mode changes may replace those targets. Preserve both current and protected original values.
  - Assign unused hotkeys in Shortcuts Details, e.g. Control-Option-Command W/E/R, not BetterDisplay Keyboard settings. Handle permissions first. Test each recipe twice for idempotence in another foreground app; inspect mode, brightness and temperature separately.
  - A missing/reidentified display is not automatically replaced by another. Partial completion is possible. Isolate failing actions, reconnect and run Restore. Stopping/deleting a shortcut does not undo completed or queued requests. Wait, restore explicit values and paused policies, then remove shortcut hotkeys/recipes if wanted; do not globally reset unrelated image adjustments.

Mode selection and color-temperature gamma controls exist (`Apps/OpenDisplay/Sources/AppModel.swift:3081`, `:705`), with temperature in kelvin rather than BetterDisplay's normalized adjustment scale. Schema fields alone do not establish presets support.
- **Implementation notes**: **L overall; M for public AppIntents orchestration.** Add persistent display entities and explicit mode/brightness/temperature actions sharing model/gateway behavior, structured results and settlement. Preserve native/DDC/software method distinctions and define FreeDisplay's own stable range contract. Temperature/advanced methods are optional extensions. TB-04–06; TB-17 for expanded ranges.
- **Acceptance criteria**:
  - Two external displays can receive independent modes and brightness; missing identities fail explicitly and never redirect to built-in/main.
  - Repeating a preset is idempotent; a failing second action reports partial completion and Restore remains usable.
  - Temperature and brightness units are unambiguous; mode settlement occurs before appearance writes; active policies and updated protection targets are observable.

- **Acceptance criteria**:
  - Remote HA reachability is an explicit opted-in capability; leaving Settings does not stop an intentionally enabled durable service, and fresh launch does not silently expose one.
  - Hardware-only requests reject software-only targets instead of dimming pixels; stable display identity is required.
  - Invalid credentials, unknown/offline display, unsupported method, accepted write and verified readback are distinguishable; none implies physical luminance confirmation.
  - Repeat requests are idempotent, unrelated clients survive configuration edits, and removing HA automation/server access does not claim rollback of prior writes.
  - Original HA setup instructions use the FreeDisplay protocol, private credentials and scoped configuration changes; no BetterDisplay endpoint compatibility is falsely promised.
